# Modern-Era Cube (180 cards, two-player)

*Designed 2026-10-01 for Justin and his coworker. Prices are the cheapest paper printing on Scryfall that day (non-promo). Every name was verified against Scryfall.*

## What it is

180 hand-picked cards from Magic's Modern era (sets from 2003 on), built for two players drafting with Winston or Grid. Ten two-color archetypes, each with two gold "signpost" cards, all five colors at 26 cards, ten artifacts, twenty dual lands. Designed around creatures and removal so games are interactive, with a few famous bombs (Sheoldred, Ragavan, The One Ring, Jace) so every draft has a "that card" moment.

Cube legality doesn't matter (it's not a sanctioned format), so Modern-banned cards like Oko, Uro, Ponder, Fury, Grief and The One Ring are in. Two cards were never printed in a Modern-legal set (Swords to Plowshares, Minsc & Boo) and are in anyway because they're cheap and great.

## How to proxy

Casual cube is where proxies are normal; nobody is entering a sanctioned event with the box. Two common routes: print at home on plain paper, cut, and sleeve each one in front of a basic land so it shuffles like a real card (free); or order custom-printed cards from a print-on-demand service such as MakePlayingCards using the MPC Autofill community tool, roughly $0.15–0.25 per card including shipping. Keep proxies for personal play only.

## The Vintage cube to proxy

The canonical list is the **MTGO Vintage Cube**, curated by Wizards and updated several times a year (current version effective 2026-08-19, about 575 cards). The official list is at mtgo.com/vintage-cube-cardlist and is mirrored on Cube Cobra as "MTGO Vintage Cube" (cubecobra.com/cube/list/mtgovc), where it can be exported as a text file for a proxy order. For two players, draft a random 180 of it each time; no trimming needed.

## Winston draft (two players)

1. Shuffle 90 cards from the cube (180 for a longer draft) into a face-down stack. Deal three single-card face-down piles next to it.
2. On your turn, look at pile 1. Take it (all its cards) or decline. If you decline, add one card from the stack to that pile face down and look at pile 2. Same choice, then pile 3. If you decline all three, take the top card of the stack blind.
3. Alternate turns until the stack and piles are gone. Build 40-card decks (basics provided), 16–17 lands.

Grid draft alternative: lay out 9 cards in a 3×3 grid, face up. Player A takes any row or column (3 cards), player B takes a remaining row or column (2–3 cards), discard the rest, repeat 18 times with the first pick alternating.
