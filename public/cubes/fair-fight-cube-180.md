# The Fair Fight Cube — flat power at every rarity (180 cards, two-player)

*Designed 2026-10-03 for Justin and a friend (Grid or Winston draft, 40-card decks, best of three). Every name was verified on Scryfall, and every card's oracle text was read there. Every card exists in Forge 2.0.14. Prices are the cheapest non-foil, non-promo paper printing on Scryfall that day (USD). Cards were picked for gameplay, and anything you don't own can be proxied.*

## What it is

The Pauper Cube's idea without its rarity rule. Pauper cubes are fun because the power ceiling is low: no card takes over a game on its own, every threat has an answer, and games are won by combat, card advantage, tempo and synergy. Rarity was only a stand-in for that. This cube keeps the low ceiling and picks cards by explicit rules instead. Commons, uncommons and rares are all in bounds, and five planeswalkers too, but only cards that pass the rules below.

The skeleton is the two-player Pauper Cube's: the same eight two-colour lanes, the same nine themes and the same speed. 99 of its 180 cards stay (its best commons and its gain lands), and every lane gets uncommons and rares that add texture rather than power: removal on a body, modal answers, build-arounds, gold signposts and creature lands.

## The flat-power rules

Every card was checked against these eight rules. A card that breaks one is out, however iconic it is (see **Rejected cards**).

1. **No single-card game-winners.** Nothing that wins if it goes unanswered for a turn or two: no Titans, no Sheoldred, no Elspeth, Sun's Champion, no Craterhoof, Jace, the Mind Sculptor or Oko. The biggest threats here are a Thragtusk, a 5/5 for three that can't attack alone (Lovestruck Beast), a 5/5 delve body or a 6/5 cycler. They are big, and a Cast Down or a chump block answers them. Card-advantage engines that grow every turn (Dark Confidant, Bitterblossom, the monarch, Wedding Announcement, equipment like Jitte and the Swords) are out too.
2. **Answers for every threat.** Every colour has removal or interaction at fair rates. White exiles (Swords, Journey, O-Ring, Skyclave Apparition, Fateful Absence). Blue counters and bounces (Counterspell, Mana Leak, Exclude, Man-o'-War, Repulse, Brazen Borrower). Black kills (Cast Down, Fatal Push, Murderous Rider, Chupacabra, Shriekmaw, edicts). Red burns (Bolt, Chain Lightning, Arc Trail, Flametongue, Fireball). Green fights (Ram Through, Primal Might) and kills artifacts and enchantments (Brontodon, Naturalists). Colourless decks get Serrated Arrows, Walking Ballista and the Mightstone. The largest creatures (Annoyed Altisaur, Krosan Tusker, Generous Ent) die to Swords, Journey, O-Ring, Cast Down, Chupacabra or Murderous Rider. All five planeswalkers have four mana value or less, so Skyclave Apparition and Oblivion Ring hit them, and so do Fateful Absence, Murderous Rider, Bone Shards, Ertai, burn and plain attacks.
3. **No combos, no fast or free mana.** No Moxen, Sol Ring, rituals, Lotus Cobra or Peregrine Drake. No infinite loops, no Stax or lock pieces (no Thalia, no Narset, Parter of Veils), and no land destruction. Mana creatures (Llanowar Elves, Wall of Roots, Werebear, Llanowar Visionary) are allowed because they die to every removal spell and only ramp one mana. Ghostly Flicker is out, so Archaeomancer has no loop to join.
4. **Card advantage in small chunks.** Two-for-ones (Mulldrifter, Chupacabra, Bonecrusher, Ertai, Skyclave), cantrips and card selection (Brainstorm, Ponder, Preordain, Augur, Faerie Seer). The biggest draw spell is Treasure Cruise (three cards). There are no five-for-ones and no engines that draw every turn for free. Tireless Tracker's and Spectral Sailor's cards cost mana each time.
5. **Rares are in for texture, not power.** Each rare is a build-around, a synergy engine, flexible or modal removal, a gold signpost, a utility land or an interesting creature with counterplay. Each one has a one-line reason in **Why each rare passes**.
6. **A few planeswalkers, all low-impact.** Five, all from the War of the Spark uncommon cycle: Davriel, Tibalt, Saheeli, Vraska and Nahiri. None has a plus ability or an ultimate. Their loyalty only goes down, so each acts like an enchantment with a static ability and two or three uses. See **The planeswalkers**.
7. **Keep Pauper's best commons.** Kor Skyfisher, Mulldrifter, Man-o'-War, Ninja of the Deep Hours, Gray Merchant, Crypt Rats, Lightning Bolt, Chain Lightning, Rancor and the gain lands still pull their weight. Weaker commons became uncommons and rares that fill the same role (Lone Missionary became Charming Prince, Dragon Fodder became Kari Zev, Owlbear became Sentinel of the Nameless City).
8. **Two-player structure.** Eight archetypes, two gold signposts per pair, nine overlapping themes. 26 cards in each colour. Every colour has a two-drop creature curve: White has Seeker, Charming Prince, Kor Skyfisher, Stoneforge and Selfless Spirit. Blue has Spellstutter, Moon-Circuit Hacker, Augur, Snapcaster, Phantasmal Image, Ledger Shredder and Thing in the Ice. Black has Carrier Thrall, Mire Triton, Bloodghast and Kitesail Freebooter. Red has Young Pyromancer, Kari Zev, Mogg War Marshal, Rimrock Knight and Embereth Shieldbreaker. Green has Satyr Wayfinder, Wall of Roots, Sakura-Tribe Elder, Scavenging Ooze, Nest Invader and Werebear. The mana is for two colours plus a splash: ten Restless creature lands (one per colour pair), eight gain lands (one per archetype pair), Evolving Wilds and Ash Barrens. Every dual enters tapped. Untapped duals plus fetchlands are left out on purpose. They make three-colour decks free, and choosing what to give up is part of drafting a flat cube.

**Rarity mix.** Each card is counted at the lowest rarity it was ever printed at in paper. 103 have been printed at common (so a Pauper cube could play them), 27 were never printed below uncommon, and 50 were never printed below rare (48 rare, 2 mythic). So 77 cards are ones a Pauper cube can't play. Gold cards come in at every rarity, and so do the colourless artifacts and the lands (the ten Restless lands are rare).

**Interaction.** 41 cards are tagged `removal`, and 5 carry `counter` (Counterspell, Mana Leak, Exclude, Spellstutter Sprite, Ertai Resurrected). White has 5 removal cards, blue 4 plus the counters, black 8, red 9, green 4, gold 8 and colourless 3. Red and black have the most, as in every cube. Green makes up for it with fight spells and big blockers.

## Decks this cube wants you to find

- **Azorius blink-skies:** Reflector Mage and Soulherder lead; Kor Skyfisher returns Mulldrifter, Man-o'-War or Skyclave Apparition to replay them; Flickerwisp, Charming Prince, Restoration Angel, Ephemerate and Momentary Blink double up; Soulherder blinks something every end step and grows.
- **Dimir ninjas:** Faerie Seer, Spellstutter Sprite, Delver, Spectral Sailor, Kitesail Freebooter and Baleful Strix connect, then Ninja of the Deep Hours, Moon-Circuit Hacker or Okiba-Gang swap in and the returned creature replays its ETB. The control version adds Counterspell, Exclude, Ertai Resurrected, Chupacabra and Gray Merchant.
- **Rakdos sacrifice:** Carrier Thrall, Nested Shambler, Mogg War Marshal, Bloodghast, Tibalt's devils and Siege-Gang's goblins as fodder; Village Rites, Woe Strider, Siege-Gang and Fling as outlets; Mayhem Devil, Falkenrath Noble and Bloodtithe Harvester as payoffs. Crypt Rats resets the board.
- **Gruul ramp:** Llanowar Elves, Wall of Roots, Sakura-Tribe Elder, Nest Invader and Writhing Chrysalis into Thragtusk, Krosan Tusker and Annoyed Altisaur, with Branching Bolt, Fireball and Rolling Thunder as the removal that scales.
- **Selesnya tokens:** Battle Screech, Secure the Wastes, Saproling Migration, Attended Knight and Lovestruck Beast go wide; Trostani Discordant, Rally the Peasants and Unbreakable Formation cash in; Qasali Pridemage handles the odd artifact.
- **Izzet spells:** cheap cantrips and burn with Swiftspear, Delver, Young Pyromancer, Guttersnipe, Ledger Shredder and Saheeli, Sublime Artificer; Thing in the Ice flips to clear the board; Snapcaster, Archaeomancer and Fire // Ice give spells back.
- **Golgari graveyard:** Mire Triton, Satyr Wayfinder, Grim Flayer and Grapple with the Past fill the yard; Gravedigger, Eternal Witness, Unearth, Pulse of Murasa and Woe Strider reuse it; Gurmag Angler and Krosan Tusker are the bodies; Vraska, Swarm's Eminence makes deathtouch assassins.
- **Boros aggro:** Fervent Champion, Swiftspear, Seeker of the Way, Kari Zev and Rimrock Knight with Bonesplitter, Shadowspear and Vulshok Morningstar; Nahiri, Storm of Stone and Lightning Helix lead; Hellrider and Rally the Peasants finish.
- **Off-lane decks:** the Restless lands and Evolving Wilds make an Orzhov drain deck (Gray Merchant, Crypt Rats, Battle Screech, Restless Fortress) or a Simic ramp-blink deck (elves, Mulldrifter, Annoyed Altisaur, Restless Vinestalk) possible.

## The planeswalkers

All five are uncommons from War of the Spark. None has a plus ability, so its loyalty only goes down, and none has an ultimate. Each is a static ability that lasts two or three activations, much like an enchantment. They are bad at protecting themselves, and any creature that attacks them shortens their life.

- **Davriel, Rogue Shadowmage** (3 mana, loyalty 3): −1 makes a player discard; it deals 2 damage each upkeep to an opponent with one card or fewer in hand. At most three discards, and it makes no blockers. It only hurts a player who is already empty-handed.
- **Tibalt, Rakish Instigator** (3 mana, loyalty 5): two 1/1 Devils that ping for 1 when they die; opponents can't gain life. The life-gain clause is the only hoser-like text in the cube, and it only lasts while Tibalt is alive. Fodder for the Rakdos deck.
- **Saheeli, Sublime Artificer** (3 mana, hybrid U/R, loyalty 5): a 1/1 Servo for each noncreature spell you cast; the −2 copy trick lasts only until end of turn. It's a Young Pyromancer that can be attacked and makes no tokens on its own.
- **Vraska, Swarm's Eminence** (4 mana, hybrid B/G, loyalty 5): two 1/1 deathtouch Assassins, and deathtouch creatures that hit a player or planeswalker grow. That's two good blockers for four mana. The Assassins also kill planeswalkers, which only matters against the other four here.
- **Nahiri, Storm of Stone** (4 mana, hybrid R/W, loyalty 6): on your turn your creatures have first strike and equip costs {1} less; −X deals X damage to a tapped creature. Each X spent on removal is loyalty gone, and she needs a board to matter.

## Why each rare passes

Cards never printed below rare. Each line says why it passes the flat-power rules.

- **Charming Prince:** a 2/2 for two with a small modal ETB (scry 2, gain 3, or blink a creature). Texture for the blink deck.
- **Stoneforge Mystic:** a build-around for equipment, but the best Equipment it can find is Shadowspear or Bonesplitter. No Jitte, Batterskull or Swords are in the cube.
- **Selfless Spirit:** a 2/1 flier that protects a board from one sweeper or one combat. Counterplay is removal in response or attacking around it.
- **Blade Splicer:** two bodies for three mana (a 1/1 and a 3/3 first striker) for the blink deck. Fair against any burn spell that kills the Splicer to turn off first strike.
- **Skyclave Apparition:** flexible removal on a 2/2 body, capped at mana value 4. If it dies, the opponent gets a token back.
- **Elite Spellbinder:** a 3/1 flier that taxes one card by {2} rather than taking it.
- **Restoration Angel:** a 3/4 flash flier that blinks one of your creatures. Value and a combat trick, but a 3/4 dies to Cast Down, Journey or Flametongue.
- **Fateful Absence:** clean instant removal that hands the opponent a Clue, so it is never a pure one-for-one.
- **Secure the Wastes:** an instant that makes X 1/1s; it scales, but it costs a card and all the mana for 1/1s that die to Crypt Rats and Sweltering Suns.
- **Unbreakable Formation:** a one-turn combat trick for go-wide decks: indestructible, plus counters and vigilance in your main phase.
- **Snapcaster Mage:** a 2/1 flash body that recasts one cheap spell, a two-for-one in small chunks.
- **Phantasmal Image:** copies the best creature on the table and dies to any targeting. Good only when the board has something worth copying.
- **Ledger Shredder:** a 1/3 flier that connives when a player casts a second spell in a turn. It grows slowly and dies to every burn spell.
- **Thing in the Ice:** a 0/4 defender that needs four instants or sorceries to flip and bounce every non-Horror creature, yours too. A build-around for the spells deck, telegraphed for turns.
- **Bloodghast:** a 2/1 that can't block and keeps coming back. Sacrifice fodder that wins nothing on its own.
- **Woe Strider:** a 3/2 plus a Goat, a free sacrifice outlet that only scries, and a slow escape. An engine piece, not a threat.
- **Murderous Rider:** modal removal (kill a creature or planeswalker, lose 2) plus a 2/3 lifelinker that goes to the bottom of the library when it dies.
- **Bomat Courier:** a 1/1 haste artifact that refills a hand only by sacrificing itself and discarding your hand.
- **Kari Zev, Skyship Raider:** a 1/3 first strike, menace two-drop whose Monkey only lasts the combat.
- **Pia Nalaar:** a 2/2 plus a 1/1 Thopter, with mana sinks that make an artifact creature hit harder or stop one blocker.
- **Bonecrusher Giant:** two damage, then a 4/3. A fair two-for-one, and it punishes the opponent for targeting it.
- **Hellrider:** a 3/3 haste for four that pings for each attacker. A finisher for go-wide aggro that dies to every removal spell and adds little on defence.
- **Siege-Gang Commander:** five mana for four bodies and a sacrifice outlet that costs {1}{R} for each 2 damage.
- **Sweltering Suns:** the cube's red sweeper (3 damage to each creature), and a cycler when it's dead. It keeps the token decks honest.
- **Fervent Champion:** a 1/1 first strike, haste one-drop that equips for {3} less. Equipment synergy on a body that dies to everything.
- **Scavenging Ooze:** graveyard interaction on a 2/2 that grows only from creature cards, at one green mana each.
- **Tireless Tracker:** a Clue for each land drop, and each Clue costs {2} to crack. Card advantage in small chunks, on a 3/2.
- **Lovestruck Beast:** a 5/5 for three that can't attack without a 1/1. It comes with a 1/1 Human, and when that dies it's a wall.
- **Thragtusk:** five life, a 5/3 and a 3/3 when it leaves. Strong against aggro, but it's a fair five-drop with no evasion.
- **Primal Might:** green's flexible removal. It needs a creature, and it's a sorcery that loses to a removal spell in response.
- **Sentinel of the Nameless City:** a 3/4 vigilance for three that makes Map tokens. Card quality, not card advantage.
- **Ertai Resurrected:** modal removal or a counter on a 3/2 flash body, and the opponent draws a card either way. A gold signpost.
- **Grim Flayer:** a 2/2 trample for two that grows to 4/4 only with four card types in your graveyard. A gold signpost.
- **Shadowspear:** +1/+1, trample and lifelink. Its other ability only matters against Selfless Spirit and Unbreakable Formation.
- **Hangarback Walker:** a mana sink that leaves Thopters when it dies. Slow, and exile removal (Swords, Journey) answers it cleanly.
- **Walking Ballista:** a flexible pinger that scales only at {4} per counter. Removal for colourless decks.
- **Solemn Simulacrum:** a land and a card on a 2/2. The model two-for-one.
- **The Mightstone and Weakstone:** five mana for draw two or −5/−5. Its mana only casts artifacts.
- **The ten Restless lands** (Anchorage, Reef, Vents, Ridgeline, Prairie, Spire, Cottage, Bivouac, Fortress, Vinestalk): duals that enter tapped and become small creatures for two to five mana. Utility lands that reward a long game and die to creature removal while animated.

The two mythics:

- **Brazen Borrower:** a tempo two-for-one (bounce, then a 3/1 flash flier that blocks only fliers). Blue's only bounce for a planeswalker.
- **Trostani Discordant:** a five-mana anthem with two lifelink 1/1s, as the GW signpost. Trostani is a legendary 1/4, so Cast Down misses it, but Swords, Journey, Fateful Absence, Murderous Rider and Chupacabra don't, and the anthem goes with it.

## Cards to watch in testing

## Forge notes

All 180 cards exist in Forge 2.0.14's card database. Three carry `AI:RemoveDeck`, rated `Random`: Bomat Courier, Soulherder, Fling. The Forge AI leaves them out of random decks but plays them when it drafts them. None is rated `All`.

Fire // Ice is keyed by Forge as a split card; the double-faced and adventure cards (Delver of Secrets, Thing in the Ice, Brazen Borrower, Murderous Rider, Bonecrusher Giant, Rimrock Knight, Embereth Shieldbreaker, Lovestruck Beast) by their front face.

## How to draft it

Grid draft is the recommended method. Lay out 9 cards from a shuffled 162 in a 3×3 grid, face up. Player A takes any row or column (3 cards). Player B takes a remaining row or column (2–3 cards). Discard the rest. Repeat 18 times, alternating who picks first.

Winston works too. Shuffle 90 (or all 180) face down and deal three one-card piles. On your turn, look at pile 1 and either take it or add a card from the stack and move to pile 2, then pile 3. If you pass all three, take the top card of the stack blind.

Build 40 cards with 17 lands (16 with two or more mana creatures). Every dual enters tapped, so keep the curve low. Play best of three, sideboarding from your drafted pool.
